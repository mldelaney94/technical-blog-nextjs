My friend recently bought some supplements they were mildly skeptical about, so I though we would review them just to see what they are for a bit of fun.

## Opti Active D

### Context

<Quote>"a form of vitamin D produced in the liver by hydroxylation of vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol) by the enzyme vitamin D 25-hydroxylase. Calcifediol can be further hydroxylated by the enzyme 25(OH)D-1α-hydroxylase, primarily in the kidney, to form calcitriol (1,25-(OH)2D3), which is the active hormonal form of vitamin D."<Ref n={1} /></Quote>

Basically this means that Vitamin D3 as we consume it, breaks down to this, and then this breaks down to the actually 'absorbed form'. So its essentially Vitamin D3 but it skips a step. Making it faster to digest and more bioavailable.

<Quote>Generally, it takes a few weeks of taking daily vitamin D supplements for vitamin D levels in the body to rise. Each 1,000 IU of vitamin D3 taken daily is expected to raise blood levels of 25(OH)D by 10 ng/ml after a few weeks. But it may take months to resolve symptoms of severe vitamin D deficiency such as rickets in children.<Ref n={2} /></Quote>

Okay, so we expect that if we're office workers who avoid the sun (like me) that A. we expect to be supplementing it all the time and B. supplementation will take some time to correct a deficiency.

In the paper "Is calcifediol better than cholecalciferol for vitamin D supplementation?" it says:

<Quote>Second, oral calcifediol is more potent than cholecalciferol, so that lower dosages are needed... Finally, intermittent intake of calcifediol results in fairly stable serum 25OHD compared with greater fluctuations after intermittent oral cholecalciferol.<Ref n={3} /></Quote>

Okay so, basically, we can just supplement normal vitamin D3 forever rather than this more expensive supplement and get the same results. If we were looking to correct it quickly, we would prefer calcifediol.

### Pregnancy/IVF

<Quote>We concluded that vitamin D supplementation should be prescribed to improve chemical pregnancy in infertile women with vitamin D deficiency and more studies are required to further confirm this finding.<Ref n={6} /></Quote>

Cool.

## Clinical lipids

Lipids are compounds that are insoluble in water like fats or steroids.

What are these lipids:

<Quote>Orthoplex Clinical Lipids 2:1 delivers a Norwegian sourced, highly concentrated dose of 1000mg combined EPA and DHA per capsule in a 2:1 ratio.<Ref n={4} /></Quote>

EPA and DHA are basically known as 'fish oils'.

In this case we really only care about the effects of fish oils on pregnancy, to whit, a paper entitled "Effect of omega-3 supplements or diets on fertility in women: A meta-analysis" says:

<Quote>This systematic review and meta-analysis suggest that omega-3 intake significantly improves women's pregnancy and fertilization rates; however, the high heterogeneity in this review somewhat limits its interpretation.<Ref n={5} /></Quote>

Heterogeneity means that the studies are not all demonstrating the same thing. Thus although on the balance of evidence there is an effect, it may be context dependent.

Cool.

## VegeNAC

Its a cysteine supplement. Cysteine is a precursor to the antioxidant glutathione. Glutathione is made from cysteine, glycine and glutamic acid,<Ref n={7} /> but glutamic acid is easily made by the body whilst cysteine and glycine are less easily made. So the paper recommends supplementing both.<Ref n={7} /> The question is, is there evidence for this with regards to pregnancy or IVF? Not that I could find. But I don't think that making more glutathione is likely to hurt either.

## Nicotinamide Riboside

Nicotinamide Riboside (or NR) is a form of vitamin B3 that the body turns into NAD+, a coenzyme that declines with age. Does it matter? This post 'aboutnad.com' (strangely specific tbh) says:

<Quote>In many cases, the underlying “why” of embryo arrest remains unknown—but recent research has begun to shed light on the biology behind it. Arrested embryos often show signs of cellular energy deficits and a quiescent- or senescent‑like state, with disrupted metabolism and stress responses that appear to stop normal development.²⁶ Given NAD+’s role in cellular energy, researchers have suggested that supporting NAD+-related pathways may improve embryo survival in the IVF setting.<Ref n={8} /></Quote>

This is not a primary paper.

Cool.

## Creatine

This one is interesting to me because creatine is supplemented due to the fact that it also increases your ATP production. But this paper from 2021 (and I can't find anything after that) says:

<Quote>there is still no consensus on whether creatine is essential for successful reproduction.</Quote>

I am flabbergasted to be honest. It may be that creatine is known as a 'gym supplement' and thus escape the eyes of science, but it is otherwise very highly studied.

I would recommend creatine supplementation if ATP production is a concern.
