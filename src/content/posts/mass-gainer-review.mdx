## Straight Up Calories
Maltodextrin, a 4 calories per gram polysaccharide (as much as straight glucose. Maybe its because its just a chain of glucose molecules so its functionally not different from a cal/g measurement perspective). This means that in order to reach my calorie goals, I could therefore drink 250g of maltodextrin a day to supplement 1000 calories in a relatively small bolus.

## Osmolality

Osmosis is just concentration gradients with membranes that are only permeable to water. This is the important part. The solute cannot move, only the water can, so what happens in the scenario where two bodies of water, one with high and one with low concentration of salt and a water permeable membrane between them start to mix? If the salt could move, it would move from high to low concentration, that's intuitive, more molecules, more movement. But as the salt CANNOT MOVE, how will the system balance the concentrations? The less salty water would have to become more salty somehow, and the saltier water would have to become less salty somehow. I.e. the less salty water moves across the membrane. Fascinating.

How does this affect our understanding of mass gainers? Well, concentration is a measure of how many molecules of a thing are in the solution. Maltodextrin is 20 or so glucose molecules stacked together that breaks down to glucose very quickly. If we shove 100,000,000 glucose molecules, and the 5,000,000 maltodextrin glucose equivalent molecules into the same body of water, we would have to conclude that the glucose molecules are more concentrated, as there are more of them in the same space. Thus, for the equivalent gram for gram amount of the carbohydrates in question, your body would need more water to osmotically balance a glucose bolus vs a maltodextrin bolus, meaning maltodextrin should make you feel less bloated whilst otherwise being pretty much the same calorifically to glucose. Fascinating.
